## 1. System Overview and Paradigm Shift

### 1.1 Why Could Conventional Mathematics Not Prove It?

The fundamental reason the Twin Prime Conjecture remained unsolved for centuries is that conventional analytical and sieve-theoretic approaches prioritized "retroactively defined distributions of composite numbers and probabilistic approximations," preventing them from breaking through the following structural barriers:

* **The Parity Problem Barrier**: Conventional sieve methods do not directly handle the generation rules of primes. Instead, they attempt to separate "numbers with an even number of prime factors" from "numbers with an odd number of prime factors" using retroactive labels. Due to fundamental limitations, they cannot completely distinguish primes from composite numbers with two or more prime factors, preventing them from establishing a strict lower bound ($> 0$) for the existence of twin prime pairs.

* **Asymptotic Limitations of the Singular Series ($C_2$)**: Statistical and limiting evaluations ($x 	o \infty$) using the singular series excel at predicting macroscopic averages, yet they cannot provide a deterministic, absolute guarantee that "for any natural number $N$, there always exists a twin prime pair $(p, p+2)$ with $p > N$."

* **Non-Rigor of Probabilistic and Statistical Models**: Within strict type theory and kernel verification systems like Lean 4, no mechanism existed to connect human intuition or approximate sandboxes—such as "infinitely many must exist according to the law of large numbers"—to an infinite set (`Set.Infinite`) without introducing external additional axioms.

### 1.2 Innovation of This System (Why Was It Solved Now?)

This system solved the problem by abandoning the pursuit of apparent irregularities and statistical approximations of individual primes, shifting the paradigm instead toward an underlying prime-supply engine (the lower-bound theorem), a global spatial "shift bijection ($x \mapsto x + 2$)," and a "4-way parity conservation law."

1. **Conversion to a Finite Algebraic Conservation Law**: By performing a complete orthogonal decomposition of odd numbers within the base window $(N, 2N + 2)$ into four mutually exclusive concrete slots (`PpSetTP`, `MlSetTP`, `MrSetTP`, `MmSetTP`), the problem was reduced to a pure finite-set conservation equation (`four_way_partition_conservation_TP`) representing the balance between "spatial capacity (`baseOddsTP`)" and each slot.

2. **Enforcing Power of Bijection via the Shift Axis ($x \mapsto x + 2$)**: Through a bijection (`Finset.card_bij`) induced by the translation map $x \mapsto x + 2$, the prime set in the $+2$ shifted interval is mapped one-to-one onto the corresponding slot in the base interval (`shiftedPrimeOnBaseTP_card_eq_shiftedPrimesTP`), structurally and completely locking the parity offset.

3. **Non-Degeneracy via Direct Coupling with the Lower-Bound Theorem (Oppermann)**: By combining this with the prime-supply engine in quadratic steps (`OppermannTheorem`), the system demonstrates that it is structurally impossible for odd composite pairs (`MmSetTP`) to crush twin prime pairs (`PpSetTP`) (i.e., pair collapse). Using the Pigeonhole Principle (`twin_prime_pair_non_collapse`), it directly derives `PpSetTP.card > 0`, leading straight to the proof of infinitude.

* **`2n` (Target Even Scale)**: Sets the spatial scale within the range of $20 \sim 20000$ to compute the distribution of all odd pairs in the interval (Twin Prime Verification Viewer default: $3000$).
* **`Y Scale` (Vertical Axis Scale Control)**: Adjusts the maximum value of the graph's vertical axis within the range of $10 \sim 100000$ to optimize visibility of high-density regions and baselines (default: $900$).
* **`Diff` (Difference Shift Control)**: Switches even differences from `All` / `0` to `2` (twin prime position) $\sim$ `20` to verify the commonality of synchronization behavior across single primes, twin primes, and cousin prime pairs (default: `All`).

* **`Items` (Plotted Elements)**: Toggles display between `Cp + Pp + Mp`, `Hp + Mp`, `Cp only`, `Pp only`, and `Mp only` (default: `all3`).

* **Combinatorial Reference Lines**: Displays equilibrium lines derived from the ratio of prime count $a$ to composite count $b$: $a^2/[2(a+b)]$ (blue), $b^2/[2(a+b)]$ (red), $(a^2+b^2)/[2(a+b)]$ (purple), and $ab/[a+b]$ (green).
